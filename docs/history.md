# Airspace Explorer — History

The finished work, moved out of `docs/plan.md` unchanged: Milestones 1-6 and Milestone 7 steps 1-6, with the measurements, lessons and decisions behind them. `docs/plan.md` holds the current plan and the rules that still shape the code. Section names such as "Step 3" refer to this file.

**Completed milestones.** Summaries only. The step-by-step history is in the git log.

**Milestone 1 — Walking skeleton (done).** One playable loop end to end:

- **Backend and data.** A FastAPI backend serves mock flights on `GET /flights` (plus `/health`), and the frontend polls it every 5 s. A failed poll keeps the last good data on screen.
- **Map and card.** Planes on a placeholder map. Clicking one opens a ticket-style card with its details, one fact and a Catch button. A selected flight that drops out of the feed stays at its last position, flagged "signal lost".
- **Quest.** One hardcoded quest in a ticket strip that gets a "Done!" stamp and a concept fact when completed. Milestone 3 turned it into one of several templates.
- **Journal.** Catches go into a Journal persisted to localStorage under a versioned key; corrupt or missing data loads as empty. Each entry stores the flight snapshot, the fact text shown and a `caughtAt` timestamp.
- **Rules that still hold:**
  - A flight can be caught once per local day.
  - Quest completion is derived from _today's_ catches, so the quest resets at local midnight (the "personal day" rule).

**Milestone 2 — Living map (done).** Moving flights and pan/zoom:

- **Real routes at real speed (backend).** The mock flights (26 hand-written between 23 real airports at the time; world-wide and generated since Milestone 7 step 6) move along great-circle routes by elapsed server time (`geo.py`) and restart from their origin on arrival with the same `id`. `duration_min` is the scheduled gate-to-gate time (air time + 25 min).
- **Smooth motion (frontend).** Planes glide between polls (5 s CSS transition) and fade in when they appear; under reduced motion they hop to each new position instead. `jumpedFlightIds` (`motion.ts`) snaps any plane that moved further than its speed allows, e.g. after a route restart or a coverage gap.
- **World map with pan/zoom.** The whole world except Antarctica in an 18:7 box with Natural Earth 1:110m coastlines (generated by `scripts/build-land.mjs`). Drag, wheel, pinch and +/−/reset buttons, 1×–16×, opening at 5× on Europe (`panzoom.ts`, `usePanZoom.ts`). Planes and line widths undo the zoom through a `--zoom` CSS variable.
- **Route and journey replay.** Selecting a flight draws its great-circle route: solid where flown, dashed ahead, with IATA chips at both airports (`RouteLayer.tsx`). "See the journey" glides the camera to fit the route, then a ghost plane flies origin → destination at about 1 s per flight hour, kept between 3 and 10 s (`replay.ts`). Closing the card or selecting another flight cancels it, and the ghost also flies under reduced motion (only the camera fit is instant).
- **Lesson: sub-pixel motion inside the zoomed layer.** The replay ghost jittered because browsers round `left`/`top` percentages to whole layer pixels _before_ the zoom is applied (steps of ~4-5 px at 4-5×, with x and y rounding independently). It is now moved with `transform: translate(x%, y%)` on a full-layer wrapper (`.ghost-track`, which must stay exactly the size of the layer). `getBoundingClientRect` hides this rounding; only screenshots show it. Anything that moves continuously inside the zoomed layer should use `transform`. Live planes still use `left`/`top`, which is fine while they move well under one layer pixel per poll.
- **Deferred idea.** Park a mock plane at its destination and fade it out before a new flight appears at the origin, which needs a "recently left the feed" state.

**Milestone 3 — Daily variety (done).** All frontend; the `/flights` contract did not change.

- **Tags (`attributes.ts`).** The one place that turns a flight into labels: altitude band (low, mid, cruise, high), airline (the 3-letter callsign prefix), region and time of day.
  - **Region.** "Land" is a point-in-polygon test on the map's own `WORLD_LAND` data. "Open ocean" is water with no land within about 3° in 8 directions. A few coarse boxes add "europe" and "north-atlantic", and big seas are excluded by box.
  - **Time of day.** Local solar time at the plane (UTC plus longitude/15), not the viewer's clock.
- **Tagged facts (`facts.ts`).** Each fact is tagged by aircraft type, airline, altitude band, region, time of day or (Milestone 6) weather. A flight gets one of the facts matching its tags, picked by a hash of flight id and local day (`hash.ts`, `dayKey` in `dates.ts`), so it is stable across polls yet varies between flights and days.
- **Quest templates (`quest.ts`).** High, low, wide-body, propeller, over open ocean, in the dark, long-haul, plus five weather ones (Milestone 6).
  - **Daily pick.** `pickQuest` starts at a point chosen by the local date and takes the first template some current flight can satisfy.
  - **Lock-in.** Only `{day, questId}` is saved (`airspace-explorer:quest:v1`), so a reload or a changed feed keeps the day's quest.
  - **Completion.** Derived from today's Journal, judging each catch at its own `caughtAt` time, which matters for the night quest.
- **Featured flights (`featured.ts`).** Three planes per session get a pulsing dashed ring: one that completes the quest, then the rarest aircraft types in the feed, one plane per type, skipping flights caught today. They are picked once when the feed and quest are ready, and a ring goes away when the flight is caught.
- **Catch scroll.** The catch that completes the quest scrolls back to the quest strip and its stamp.

**Milestone 4 — Collection, badges and the catch moment (done).** All frontend.

- **Collection screen (`Collection.tsx`).** Journal, Badges and Facts tabs behind a header button, a plain `view` state in `App.tsx`, no router. The Journal state lives in `JournalContext.tsx` (provider, store and consumer hook in separate files, one hook per file, to avoid a React Fast Refresh lint warning).
- **Badges (`badges.ts`).** First catch, 5 distinct aircraft types, 3 different quest-completion days, and a catch over open ocean, each a pure function over the Journal. `JournalEntry.questId` (optional) is set only on the catch that completed that day's quest. Only the set of badges already shown a popup persists (`badgesSeen.ts`); unlocked state is derived (`useBadgeUnlocks.ts`), and a new badge shows a one-time toast (`BadgePopup.tsx`).
- **Passport card.** The flight card is a passport page. Catch is one uniform, immediate reaction for every flight ("Caught today ✓"). A per-flight stamp animation with a delay before the card closed was tried and simplified away, since it added complexity without improving the feel. Later (2026-10-09) the card stopped closing on Catch at all: the button simply becomes the stamp, which fixes the old problem that the card vanished before the stamp could be seen.
- **Entry stamp and Journal.** Each entry is a small ink stamp (`EntryStamp.tsx`), destination-focused (the callsign when there is no route), laid out as a wrapping row.
- **Facts tab.** `FactList.tsx` lists every distinct fact learned so far, in the order first caught.

**Milestone 5 — Visual pass, mascot and onboarding (done).** The retro travel-poster kawaii identity. Frontend only; details are in `docs/ui-foundation.md`.

- **Map art.** `WorldLand.tsx` reskins the coastlines (north-lit gradient, zoom-compensated shadow, dotted graticule, CSS sea texture). `CountryBorders.tsx` adds borders and name labels at a constant screen size with collision avoidance using the map's rendered width.
- **Aircraft silhouettes and time-of-day palette.** `aircraftSilhouette.ts` sorts ICAO types into 5 families, drawn by `PlaneIcon.tsx`. The map's sea and land palette follows the solar time at the viewport centre and eases over 0.9 s.
- **Mascot.** `Mascot.tsx`, a chibi cat pilot with `idle`/`talk`/`cheer` faces. It delivers the quest-completion fact, once a day.
- **Onboarding.** `useOnboarding.ts` is a `greeting` -> `catch` -> `done` state machine, run once per device. The mascot speaks in a typed-out bubble, and the catch step rings one real flight near the default view (`onboardingTarget.ts`, pinned once picked) but completes on _any_ catch.
- **Framer Motion** is a real dependency now (card entrance, stamp, badge popup, onboarding). Under `prefers-reduced-motion` the converted components appear and unmount at once, by switching off their initial and exit animations.
- **Testing gotcha.** Under Vitest's fake timers a `motion` exit transition can fail to complete after earlier tests drove other fake-timer animations. Mocking `matchMedia` to report reduced motion in the affected tests (the `reducedMotion()` helper in `App.test.tsx`, per test) fixes it. The journey-replay tests need `prefersReducedMotion()` to read `false`.
- **Known limits.** The onboarding target is the flight nearest the default view; with a sparse live feed it could be off-screen. On a very short viewport the docked mascot and hint overlap the card's text until you scroll. Not checked on a real phone.

**Milestone 6 — Weather (done).** Backend plus frontend; the `/flights` contract did not change.

- **Real source, mock data.** The intended real source is the Aviation Weather Center's global SIGMET feed (`https://aviationweather.gov/api/data/isigmet?format=json`): free, no key, about 100 requests/min and 400 results per query. It was checked against a live response. It is worldwide (about 100 advisories), unlike the AWC's `airsigmet` and G-AIRMET products, which are US-only. Open-Meteo was the alternative and was dropped (dense but not aviation-specific). Why SIGMETs rather than METAR or TAF: `docs/aviation-weather-products.md`.
  - **Fields seen:** `icaoId`, `firId`, `firName`, `validTimeFrom`/`validTimeTo` (epoch seconds), `seriesId`, `hazard` (TS, TURB, ICE, MTW, VA, TC), `qualifier`, `base`/`top` (feet), `geom` ("AREA"), `coords` (lat/lon points), `dir`, `spd`, `chng`, `rawSigmet`.
  - **Checked in Milestone 7, step 4** against a full live response (162 advisories); see that step for what the feed really holds.
- **Backend.** `GET /weather` returns `WeatherArea`s: `id`, `hazard` (thunderstorm, turbulence, icing, volcanic_ash, tropical_cyclone), `region`, altitude band, `valid_from`/`valid_to` (Unix seconds), a closed `polygon` and optional movement. It is a separate endpoint because a hazard belongs to an area, not to one plane, and flights and weather will come from different sources. The mock (`mock_weather.py`) was a static snapshot of 9 rectangular areas (18 since Milestone 7 step 6) whose validity is recomputed around "now".
- **Icons (`WeatherLayer.tsx`, `HazardIcon.tsx`, `weatherIcons.ts`).** Round stickers under the planes: one icon for a small area, up to 4 as you zoom in, placed inside the polygon. The outline and label appear when an icon is clicked. A ☁ button hides or shows all weather.
- **Game attribute.** `weatherAt` (`attributes.ts`) says a flight is "in" a hazard when it is inside the polygon, within the altitude band and the advisory is valid. A flight above an icing layer is not in it. Five weather quest templates, ten weather facts (shown only for a flight inside a hazard), and the quest-completing featured flight can be a weather one.
- **Bugs fixed.**
  - A weather quest could un-complete when the live weather moved away. "Done" is now the saved `questId` match, or the quest still matching.
  - Quest picking raced the weather request, so weather quests were never picked that day. `useWeather` is `null` until its first request settles (a failure settles to "no weather"), and the quest and featured flights wait for it.
- **Tried and dropped:** a weather tint over hazard areas (muddy smudges, invisible over sea) and a global palette shift (too heavy for 9 small areas). Region and weather palette shifts remain deferred.
- **Known limits.**
  - With the mock, few flights are ever inside a hazard, so a weather quest is often skipped (icing is the hardest).
  - A quest saved for the day can outlive its hazard once the weather is live.
  - A flight's fact can change between polls as it moves in or out of an area.
  - Planes are drawn above weather icons. The icon count assumes a map about 1000 px wide.

**Milestone 7 — Live data and deployment:** real flights and weather, publicly hosted. Done one step at a time.

**Order of the remaining steps:** 5 quests, 6 world-wide mock data, 7 deployment (steps 3 density and 4 real weather are done). Density came first because it ties to the stutter measured below and changes what the other steps look like. The quest review follows weather because real weather changes which weather quests can match.

**Step 1a — OpenSky provider (backend only, done).** Run live once by the user on 2026-10-04.

- **Quota (re-verified 2026-10-04).** 4,000 credits per day for a standard account, no monthly cap found. A global `/states/all` costs 4 credits, and so does any box over 400 sq°, so a bounding box saves nothing. `X-Rate-Limit-Remaining` reports what is left; at zero the API answers 429 with `X-Rate-Limit-Retry-After-Seconds`.
- **Provider.** `providers.py` holds the `FlightProvider` Protocol and `create_provider`. `FLIGHT_SOURCE=mock` (default) or `opensky`, so a missing secret never breaks the app. `opensky.py` has the token handling, mapping, cache and dead reckoning. One shared snapshot refreshes on demand after 90 s, so no visitors means no quota use. On failure it serves the stale snapshot and backs off (30 s, or as long as a 429 asks).
- **Credit guard** (normal preset): a 60 s refresh floor, a 200-credit hourly cap and a 400-credit reserve read from OpenSky's header. `/health` shows its state. The presets and arithmetic are in `docs/opensky-credit-guard.md`.
- **Mapping.** `id` is the callsign, or the `icao24` address when there is none (the callsign then shows as "Unknown"). On-ground aircraft and incomplete rows are dropped, and duplicate callsigns keep the freshest report. The whole world is served, capped by a hash-of-id sample (1,500 at first; since step 3's second pass a 15,000 safety ceiling, so the whole world). That keeps the altitude mix (every quest can still match) and the same planes between refreshes. It is uniform, so it mirrors real traffic: Europe full, oceans, Africa and South America sparse. **Decided: keep it uniform.** A random pick each refresh was rejected because planes would flicker and selection, the once-a-day catch rule and fact choice would shift.

**Step 1b — Readability and performance of live data (done except density).**

- **Journey readability (done 2026-10-04, found by the user with live data).** Frontend only (details and the CSS gotcha are in `docs/ui-foundation.md`).
  - The route line is bent through the plane (`curveThrough`), because real planes are off the great-circle arc (airways, ocean tracks, winds; the plausibility check allows up to about 25%) and the solid and dashed parts used to meet beside the plane. Unchanged for a plane on the arc.
  - With a flight selected the other planes dim, during the replay they disappear, the route has a paper outline above the other planes (under the selected one), and planes shrink when zoomed out.
- **Motion (measured 2026-10-04, no code change).** Planes moving in fits and starts is a rendering cost, not timing. Headless Chromium (software rendering, so absolute numbers are pessimistic) with planes flown on every poll: polls arrive steadily every ~5.0 s; 30 planes glide at 60 fps and 600 at ~57 fps, but 1,500 drop to ~32 fps with about 50 frames over 50 ms in 20 s, against ~57 fps with the glide switched off. Moving the glide to the `translate` property (positions in `cqw`/`cqh`) did not help (24-37 fps, noisy) and was reverted; `will-change`, dropping the fade-in and dropping the icon's shadow made no clear difference. The cost grows with the number of planes, so the real fix is drawing fewer of them (step 3). Not tested: a real GPU or a phone. Method: Playwright with N injected moving planes, measuring `requestAnimationFrame` deltas over 20 s plus the gaps between polls (the script lived in a scratchpad, not the repo).
- **UI credit (done).** A small line under the map names OpenSky, its aircraft database and adsbdb.

**Step 2 — Aircraft type, route and duration for live flights (done).** Run against fakes, and the route service against the real adsbdb with a few requests. Not verified: the full flow against the live feed in a browser by Claude (the user ran it live and liked the card).

- **Sources (spike 2026-10-04).**
  - OpenSky's `/flights/*` endpoints are historical, so they cannot give a live route.
  - adsbdb (`api.adsbdb.com`, free, no key, MIT-licensed code) answered in 0.1-0.3 s: `GET /v0/aircraft/{icao24}` (type, ICAO type code, manufacturer, registration), `GET /v0/callsign/{callsign}` (route with full airport details), and `GET /v0/aircraft/{icao24}?callsign={callsign}` for both. It returns 404 when anything asked for is unknown, **including the combined call when only one half is unknown**, so a combined 404 is followed by one request per half. It documents no rate limit, no bulk endpoint and no uptime promise. Its README restricts the route data ("may not be copied, published, or incorporated into other databases"), and the MIT licence covers the code, not the data. The aircraft data comes from PlaneBase with no stated terms.
  - OpenSky's aircraft database is a 106 MB CSV (newest snapshot January 2025). Its page calls it "unlicensed", "as is", and asks that publications (web pages included) cite Schäfer et al., "Bringing Up OpenSky", IPSN 2014. 83% of its 601,282 aircraft have a type code. Against one live snapshot (10,811 airborne usable planes), 82% had a type; of the 1,968 without, 1,697 were not in the database (newer aircraft) and 271 had no type code.
- **Type.** A compact `icao24 -> type code` file (`backend/app/data/aircraft_types.csv.gz`, 501,934 aircraft, 1.8 MB) is committed and loaded at startup. `backend/scripts/build_aircraft_types.py` rebuilds it (steps in `docs/opensky-aircraft-database.md`). A plane not in it has `aircraft_type = null`; the log line shows how many kept flights have a type. A tap also fills in the type of a plane missing from the file.
- **Route, on a tap only.** `GET /flights/{id}/route` finds the flight in the current snapshot and asks adsbdb (`routes.py`). It answers 404 for an unknown flight or nothing known, and 503 when adsbdb is down; the frontend treats both as "unknown". A flight with a route in the feed (the mock) is answered from the feed. Answers are kept **in memory only**, never written to disk, because of the route data restriction: 24 h for a hit, 1 h for "unknown", failures not cached. The route is checked against the plane's position on **every** lookup (the plane must lie roughly between the airports), because a callsign can be flown on another leg later in the day. An earlier version checked only on the first lookup and served the cached route for the wrong plane; this was caught against the live adsbdb.
- **Duration is an estimate:** great-circle distance at 440 kt plus 25 minutes of ground time. `Flight.duration_estimated` marks it, and the card shows "~7h 13m" in a dashed chip with a tooltip.
- **Contract.** `aircraft_type` and `duration_min` are optional, and the `"UNKNOWN"`/`1` placeholders are gone. The mock still fills every field.
- **Long-haul quest is mock-only.** Live flights carry no duration in the feed, so `pickQuest` can never pick it on live data (a test pins this down). The featured rings always work, since they use attributes every flight has.
- **Frontend.** The selected flight is looked up when tapped (`useFlightRoute.ts`, `route.ts`) and merged in at render time, not into the polled snapshot, so a poll does not erase it. The card says "Looking up route…", then fills in, or says "Route unknown" and hides the type, duration, route and journey button. The merged flight is what the route line, the replay and the Catch snapshot use. Quests, facts, badges, featured flights and silhouettes treat a null type as "unknown".
- **Extra detail (after step 2).** Everything the two sources give that the card did not use, except photos.
  - From OpenSky, for every plane: climb or descent rate ("↗ 1,900 ft/min", "level" under 300) and the country of registration.
  - From adsbdb, on a tap, in `FlightRoute`: airline and its country, tail number, manufacturer, model, the registered owner (shown as "Operated by" only when it is not the airline) and the airports' city and country. Display only, never used by quests, facts or badges. The airline survives a route rejected by the plausibility check, since it comes from the callsign.
  - Left out: OpenSky's squawk code (emergency codes need careful handling), the airport elevation and OpenSky's category field.
- **Photos: left out (decided 2026-10-04).** adsbdb gives a photo URL (airport-data.com); a "See a photo" link was built, tried and removed because the photo source was not good enough. The OpenSky database has no photo links, and collecting links in bulk from adsbdb would breach its data restriction. **Planespotters.net** is the candidate to revisit: a public, rate-limited lookup by aircraft address (`api.planespotters.net/pub/photos/hex/{icao24}`) that requires a User-Agent with a real contact URL or email, a "© photographer" credit and a link back. Not verified: its terms (read them in a browser) and the response shape. Revisit after deployment, when there is a project URL to use as the contact, or together with the 3D-model idea.
- **Known limits.**
  - Type coverage is about 82% on the map; the rest show a default silhouette and cannot match type quests until tapped. The file dates from January 2025, so rebuild it when OpenSky publishes a newer snapshot.
  - adsbdb is a volunteer service with no documented limits. Only taps use it, and every failure hides the fields.
  - Routes come from the callsign, so a wrong or outdated one is possible, and the estimate can be off by tens of minutes.
  - A tapped flight's fact can change when the lookup adds its type.

**Step 3 — Density (done 2026-10-04).** Option E in earlier notes. The live map is very full, and the motion measurement above ties the stutter to the plane count (1,500 markers is about 32 fps; 600 is about 57). Frontend only, no credits.

- **Built, first pass.** `visiblePlanes.ts` (`visibleFlights`), used by `FlightMap.tsx`: only planes in view (5% margin), thinned by a stable id hash to about 150 at world zoom, growing with zoom. Measured with 1,500 moving planes (headless Chromium, software rendering, 20 s each): opening Europe view 794 planes at 57 fps, world view 118 at 60, zoomed in 297 at 59.
- **Second pass: sparse regions (found live by the user).** Zoomed in on Australia, Asia, Africa or South America the map looked sparse. The cause was the server's world-wide cap (1,500 of about 10,800 airborne planes is 14%), not the frontend cull: once zoomed in the frontend already drew everything it was sent. FlightRadar24 caps what is shown **per view** (a forum post says 1,500 at once, sorted by altitude; unofficial), not per world.
  - **Payload measured** (synthetic live-like planes, the real field set): 1,500 planes 0.43 MB (0.05 MB gzipped), 4,000 planes 1.16 MB (0.14 MB), 10,800 planes 3.13 MB (0.37 MB). The server did not compress before; gzip is about 8x.
  - **Backend (final).** `DEFAULT_MAX_FLIGHTS = 15000` in `opensky.py`, a ceiling above the whole world (about 10,800 airborne), so by default every plane is sent. It first went in at 5,000, a cautious midpoint, until the measurements below showed the full feed is fine. `OPENSKY_MAX_FLIGHTS` still changes it, and the hash sample keeps the same planes between refreshes if a lower cap is set. `GZipMiddleware` in `main.py` compresses the list.
  - **Frontend.** `visibleFlights` now draws at most `VIEW_CAP = 800` planes in view. If more are in view, a plane is kept when its id hash is under `800 / count in view`, so a plane keeps one fixed rank and zooming in only adds planes. 800 because 794 drawn planes held 57 fps while 1,500 gave 32; the extra planes from the server only fill emptier regions. A small feed (the mock) is drawn whole. Selected, featured and onboarding-target planes are always drawn; quest picks still use the full feed.
  - **Measured with 5,000 moving planes** (same method): opening Europe view 669 drawn at 57.5 fps, world view 716 at 57.3, zoomed in 791 at 57.0.
  - **Measured with the full 10,800** (cap on): server 109 ms per `/flights` request (60 ms at 5,000; validate, serialise and gzip, no dead reckoning), frontend cull 0.85 ms per render, JSON parse 9 ms for 2.2 MB on a laptop; drawn 826, 890 and 787 planes at 56, 54 and 56 fps in the three views.
  - **"All planes" switch: tried and removed (2026-10-04).** A map-control switch that lifted the draw cap was built, then removed by the user's decision. Measured with 10,800 moving planes (headless software rendering) it drew 5,705 planes at 2.9 fps in the opening Europe view, 10,800 at 1.2 fps in the world view and 2,096 at 23 fps zoomed in, so it was close to unusable. The 800 draw cap stays. Where fewer than 800 planes are in view (sparse regions zoomed in) nothing is thinned, so every plane the server sent is drawn. A zoom-gated variant (lift the cap only below about 1,500 in view) was weighed and not built.
  - **Zooming in shows everything.** The 800 limit is on the planes in view, so each zoom step (a quarter of the area) lifts the thinning once 800 or fewer are in view. A rough, unmeasured estimate puts Europe's opening view at about 2,000-3,000 planes, so it would be fully drawn around zoom 9 of 16. The very densest spots (the London-Paris-Frankfurt corridor at peak) might still exceed 800 at the maximum zoom; not checked.
  - **To check live (user):** whether Australia, Asia, Africa and South America now look right, whether Europe's opening view is too dense, how many planes are really in view before the cap (a temporary log would give it), and the poll size and server time on a real feed. The cap is one setting (`OPENSKY_MAX_FLIGHTS`) and one constant (`VIEW_CAP`).
  - **Known limits.** Unmeasured: the JSON parse and React work on a phone, and dead reckoning time on the server for the whole world. Where more than 800 planes are in view the cut is by hash rank, so which planes show is arbitrary.

- **Decided 2026-10-04 (approach C, refined in the second pass).** Viewport cull plus a cap on the planes in view (first a cap that grew with zoom, then a flat 800 in view); balanced priority (smooth at world zoom, fuller as you zoom in); no marker spacing; target about 55 fps or more in the repeated measurement.
- **Approach.**
  - Draw only the planes in the visible area, at most 800 at once (second pass; the first pass used a cap that grew with zoom from about 150).
  - The cap is filled by a stable hash of the flight id, so planes do not flicker when panning (one plane, one fixed rank). A "most interesting first" ranking (rare types, extremes, weather) is left for later.
  - Quest, featured, selected and onboarding-target planes are always shown. Quest and featured picks are still made from the full feed, so a server-side or visible-area filter cannot starve them.
  - Overlapping markers stay as they are (decided: like FlightRadar24, a crowded sky is fine).
  - Must keep working with the glide, the jump snap and the route/replay dimming in `FlightMap.tsx`.
- **Alternatives weighed.** A per-grid-cell cap on the server (B) thins Europe but reverses "keep it uniform"; a learning-first selection (A) needs good type data. Neither is planned now.
- **Done when:** the plane-count measurement is repeated and the map holds about 55 fps or more (done with 1,500, 5,000 and 10,800 planes sent), panning and zooming do not make planes pop in and out distractingly, and the selected, quest and featured planes are never hidden.
- **Nice to have, out of scope for this step.**
  - A "most interesting first" ranking (rare types, extremes, weather) in place of the hash order.
  - Clustering or count badges in dense areas.
  - Cheaper rendering (a canvas or SVG layer) if the DOM markers are still too heavy, for example on a phone.
  - Server-side thinning (per grid cell), which would reverse "keep it uniform".
  - A per-view server request (`/flights?bbox=`, like FlightRadar24 may do) or a hybrid base-plus-detail feed, if sending the whole world proves too heavy (payload, server time or a phone). Weighed in the second pass; not needed yet.
  - Testing on a real GPU and a phone (the measurements so far are headless software rendering).

**Step 4 — Real weather (done 2026-10-05).** A provider behind the unchanged `/weather` contract, fed by the AWC international SIGMET feed. Why SIGMETs rather than METAR or TAF: `docs/aviation-weather-products.md`. Checked against a full live response and mapped end to end in tests; the user ran it live in a browser and said the map looks good. Not verified by Claude: the full flow in a browser.

- **The feed (read 2026-10-05).** AWC allows 100 requests/min and 400 entries per query, forbids cross-origin calls (only the backend calls it) and errors on undocumented query parameters, so the request is `?format=json` and nothing else. It asks for a custom User-Agent: `AirspaceExplorer/0.1` (no contact, the repo is private; `WEATHER_USER_AGENT` overrides it, and a project URL can go there after step 7).
  - **Contents.** 162 advisories from 69 FIRs: thunderstorm 83, turbulence 41 (9 of them mountain wave), icing 20, volcanic ash 11, tropical cyclone 7. An earlier summary said 289 entries and was wrong, so recount from the raw JSON, not from a summary.
  - **Validity.** Each advisory has its own times: 2-4 h for thunderstorms, turbulence and icing, about 6 h for cyclones and ash. The "issued hourly, valid 2 hours" wording on the AWC site describes US convective SIGMETs, which this app does not use.

A malformed or unusable advisory is skipped and logged, and the log line counts what was dropped. A response near the 400 cap logs a warning.

**Code review fixes before deployment (2026-10-07).** A full review produced a list; the items that matter for the public site are done one at a time, each its own `[fix]` commit.
- **CORS from the environment.** `CORS_ORIGINS` (comma-separated) replaces the hard-coded Vite origin; the default is still the two localhost origins. Set it, and `VITE_API_URL` on the frontend, when deploying.
- **Validated data.** Flights, weather areas, route lookups, Journal entries and seen-badge ids are checked with small hand-written guards (`validate.ts`). A bad row is dropped and the rest kept, a response that is not a list fails the request, and the number dropped is logged once per change. A schema library was 40-100x slower on a 15,000-plane feed in a benchmark. A Journal entry is dropped only when it lacks what every entry has, so older entries with fewer details are kept; a dropped one is gone once the Journal is next saved.
- **The flight feed failing.** Polling no longer overlaps, a request times out after 30 s (body included), failures back off by skipping ticks, and after 3 in a row the banner says the planes may be out of date and offers "Try again". A first load that fails shows "Could not load flights" with the same button, and the quest banner says today's quest will appear once flights load. Weather retries (30 s, doubling up to 5 min) and its switch is hidden while there is none.
- **Route lookups.**
  - *Backend.* The cache used to stop caching at 2,000 entries; it now drops the entry closest to expiring. A failed adsbdb call pauses new requests for 15 s (counted from the failure, since a timeout alone takes 8 s) and cached answers are still served. At most 8 requests run at once, and a lookup that finds no free slot within 2 s gives up without starting the pause.
  - *Frontend.* An answer is reused for 30 minutes (so "unknown" no longer lasts until a reload), and the old answer stays on screen while a new lookup runs, and is kept if it fails. A plane is looked up only after 250 ms selected, and deselecting cancels the lookup. After a failure, selecting the plane again shows "loading" instead of the old failure.
  - *Found in review of my own change:* the card could get stuck on "loading" once an open card's answer passed 30 minutes, because staleness was checked in render but only acted on when the plane changed. Showing the old answer whatever its age fixed it, with a test.
  - *Known limit.* Cancelling stops the browser waiting, not the backend, which finishes its adsbdb call. The cap and the 250 ms wait keep a burst of taps small; the rest is in "Smaller ideas".
- **Not verified:** the live OpenSky and AWC payloads against the guards, and the 15 s pause and retry flow against real adsbdb or in a browser.

- **Blurry map when zooming on an iPhone (found on a real iPhone 13).** The map was one layer scaled up with CSS (`scale(zoom)`, up to 16x); the phone browser kept the sharpness the layer had when it was drawn, so zooming in only stretched a picture, and it stayed blurry after lifting the fingers. Two changes fixed it (confirmed on the iPhone and on desktop): the world layer is now drawn `WORLD_SCALE` = 3 times larger than the map box and scaled down by the same factor (`--zoom` is the scale actually applied, `--real-zoom` the view's zoom), and on touch screens the maximum zoom is 9 instead of 16 (raised from 6, which was sharp on the iPhone; 9 is untested and can be lowered if the blur returns) (`TOUCH_MAX_ZOOM`). - **Portrait map (to be checked on the phone).** On a phone held upright (`orientation: portrait` and under 600 px wide) the map box is 4:3 instead of the 18:7 strip, about twice as tall. For that, pan and zoom no longer assume the world exactly fills the box: `panzoom.ts` has a `Shape` (`lx`, `ly`, how many box widths and heights the world spans at zoom 1), worked out from the box's measured size (`shapeFor`). A box within 10% of the world's 18:7 counts as the same shape (`WHOLE`, lx = ly = 1), so desktop and landscape behave as before. In a 4:3 box lx is about 1.9: the whole north-south extent shows but only about half the longitudes, and you cannot zoom out to the whole world, as in any phone map app. The shape reaches the clamps, `centredOn`, `fitBounds` (journey replay), `centreOf` (time-of-day palette), the visible-planes filter, the country-label collision check (which now gets the real box height instead of assuming 18:7) and the world layer's size. The start view lowers its zoom by the same factor so about as much of Europe is in view as on a wide map, and a map nobody has moved reopens on the start view when the shape becomes known or the phone is rotated (a moved one keeps the world point at its centre and the same span of longitudes in view, so rotating does not lose your place; found on the phone, the first version only clamped it). Not covered by tests: how it looks and feels. Tune the 4:3 (`aspect-ratio` in the portrait rule of `index.css`) after trying it.
- **Services made on demand (review item 7).** `main.py` built the providers when the file was imported: a bad `FLIGHT_SOURCE` crashed the import, a shell with `FLIGHT_SOURCE=opensky` made `test_health` use the live provider, the HTTP clients were never closed, and the tests patched module globals. Now `get_provider`, `get_weather_provider` and `get_route_service` are `Once` objects (a service made on first use and kept, behind a lock) that the endpoints take with `Depends()`, a `lifespan` makes them at startup (so a bad setting stops the server with the clear message) and closes them at shutdown (`close()` on the providers and `RouteService`, closing only an HTTP client the object made itself, and one failing close does not stop the others), and tests swap a service with `app.dependency_overrides`. `tests/conftest.py` pins mock sources and clears the cache and the overrides around every test, and drops a shell `CORS_ORIGINS` before the app is imported; the suite passes with `FLIGHT_SOURCE=opensky`, `WEATHER_SOURCE=nope` and `CORS_ORIGINS` set in the shell. Checked with a real uvicorn: start, `/health`, a route and `/weather` work, and a bad `FLIGHT_SOURCE` or missing OpenSky credentials stop startup with the message. Also run under a real uvicorn with the live provider classes (fake credentials, no network): the three services exist before the first request, `/health` answers, and a graceful shutdown (what Ctrl+C triggers) closes all three HTTP clients and empties the cache. Not checked: the live OpenSky and AWC feeds themselves. Creation is thread-safe, so two requests arriving together before a service exists (for instance under `uvicorn --lifespan off`) cannot each make one (two OpenSky providers would each spend credits); a test starts 8 threads at once and fails without the lock.
- **Live code no longer loads the mock data, and the route handler only maps errors (review items 8 and 9).** `routes.py` took `GROUND_MIN` from `mock_flights`, and importing it built the 100 mock flights; but the root coupling was bigger: `providers.py` (home of `ProviderError`, imported by every live module) imported the mock data at the top, so `opensky`, `awc` and `routes` all loaded it too. Now `GROUND_MIN` is in `constants.py`, the two mock providers are in `mock_providers.py` and `create_provider`/`create_weather_provider` import it only for the mock source (like the live ones), and a test imports the live modules in a fresh interpreter and checks that no mock module was loaded (it fails if the coupling returns). The handler's "the feed already has this route" branch moved into `RouteService.lookup` (`route_from_feed`), so the handler only finds the flight, calls `lookup` and turns `LookupUnavailable` into 503 and no route into 404.
- **Bug found by the user: a route across the date line was drawn as a line across the whole map.** A flight such as Auckland to Bogota (174.8 E to 74 W) has points whose longitude jumps from +180 to -180, and `RouteLayer` projected them one by one into one polyline, so the step between two neighbouring points was drawn straight across the map (a horizontal line near the bottom). `splitAtDateLine` in `geo.ts` now splits the path where it crosses: each part ends exactly on its edge and the next starts on the other edge at the same latitude (found by interpolating between the two points), and longitudes a bent curve pushes past 180 count as the other side. The solid, dashed and replay-trail lines are all drawn in parts. Tests check eastbound and westbound crossings, the crossing latitude, back-and-forth paths and the rendered lines (they fail without the fix). **Known limits:** the replay camera frames the bounds of the route's projected points, so for a date-line route it frames the whole width (the middle of the world in a tall box, not the route); and the ghost plane jumps from one edge to the other when it crosses, as planes do on this map, which does not wrap.
- **One snapshot cache for OpenSky and AWC (review item 10).** Both providers had the same machinery: a lock so one refresh runs at a time, a snapshot with its fetch time, a refresh when it is old, and after a failure a back-off (the wait a 429 asked for, else a fixed one) while the old snapshot is served, raising only when there is none. The two `RateLimited` classes (same name, different types) are one in `providers.py`, with the same message text.
